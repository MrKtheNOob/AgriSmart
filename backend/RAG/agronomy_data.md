Understanding the soil is the first step in providing accurate agronomic

practical

\## 15

The Tirs (Vertisols) are arguably the most significant agricultural soils in
the plains of Gharb,

Saïs, and Chaouia.

\## 12

Their high clay content, specifically smectite, allows for high water

retention but makes them difficult to manage.

\## 17

During the dry season, these soils contract,

forming deep vertical cracks that can reach over a meter in depth.

\## 15

Conversely, when wet,

they expand and become nearly impermeable, posing risks of surface waterlogging
during

heavy winter rains.

\## 16

Their natural fertility makes them ideal for industrial crops and intensive

cereal production, provided the tillage is conducted within the narrow moisture
range where

the soil is neither too sticky nor too hard.

\## 17

Hamri soils represent a more versatile medium for many Moroccan farmers. Often
situated on

calcareous tuffs, they provide a balanced environment for both cereals and
fruit trees like

olives and almonds.

\## 15

Their reddish hue indicates a high level of iron oxidation, and they

typically offer better drainage than Tirs, reducing the risk of root
asphyxiation in sensitive

species.

\## 16

R'mel soils, predominant in coastal regions such as the Loukkos, present a
different set of

challenges and opportunities.

\## 12

Their sandy texture allows for rapid infiltration and quick

warming in the spring, making them the preferred choice for early-season market
gardening

\## (primeurs).

\## 16

However, their low capacity to retain water and nutrients necessitates frequent

irrigation and careful fertilization to prevent groundwater contamination.

\## 18

Soil-Water Dynamics and Hydric Regimes

The hydric profile of these soils is governed by the Mediterranean climate,
characterized by a

concentration of rainfall in the winter months followed by a severe summer
drought.

\## 5

\## In Tirs

soils, the profile is often fully humidified by early winter, as water
penetrates rapidly through

vertical cracks.

\## 16

In contrast, R'mel soils exhibit two superimposed hydric regimes: a surface

sandy layer that dries quickly and a deeper clayey horizon that may remain
moist throughout

the summer, supporting deep-rooted perennials.

\## 16

Recent research emphasizes the importance of "Semis Direct" (No-Till) as a
mechanism to

preserve soil moisture in these varied environments.

\## 13

By maintaining a cover of crop residues,

farmers can reduce soil temperature and minimize evaporation, which is crucial
given that up

to $40\\%$ of annual precipitation can be lost to surface evaporation in
conventional tillage

systems.

\## 16

Cereal Resilience: The Foundation of Food Security

Cereals occupy the majority of Morocco's arable land, with Durum Wheat (Blé
dur), Soft

Wheat (Blé tendre), and Barley (Orge) forming the dietary and economic base for
millions.

\## 11

Durum and Soft Wheat (Blé Dur & Blé Tendre)

Wheat production in Morocco is almost entirely rainfed and thus highly
susceptible to

inter-annual rainfall variability.

\## 23

The 2025/26 season has seen a favorable start, but long-term

trends indicate a persistent deficit that requires the adoption of
drought-tolerant varieties.

\## 5

● Environmental Requirements: Wheat thrives in regions with $350 - 600 mm$ of

annual rainfall.

\## 9

Blé tendre is generally more resistant to cold than Blé dur, which

requires higher levels of sunshine and warmth to achieve optimal grain quality.

\## 9

\## The

preferred soil texture is loamy-clay (limono-argileuse), which provides the
depth and

drainage necessary for root development.

\## 9

● Climate Thresholds: The "zero of vegetation" for wheat is approximately
$4^\\circ C$,

with germination ideally occurring at temperatures between $18^\\circ C$ and
$20^\\circ

\## C$.

\## 9

Extreme heat, particularly the "Chergui" during the grain-filling stage, can
lead to

"échaudage," where the grain shrivels, significantly reducing the
thousand-grain weight

(PMG) and overall yield.

\## 22

● Varietal Innovation: INRA has introduced varieties such as Nachit and Jawaher
(Blé

dur) and Lina (Blé tendre), which have demonstrated high stability and yields
exceeding

50 qx/ha under experimental conditions.

\## 4

These varieties are selected for their

resistance to major diseases like rust (rouille) and oïdium, as well as their
ability to

withstand the terminal drought prevalent in Moroccan semi-arid zones.

\## 4

Barley (Orge): The Hardy Alternative

Barley remains the crop of choice for Morocco's most marginal lands in the
Oriental and

Souss regions.

\## 11

Its shorter growing cycle and superior water-use efficiency allow it to

produce a crop with as little as $200 mm$ of rain.

\## 22

The variety Hydroponica, recently added

to the national catalog, highlights the move toward intensive fodder production
to support the

livestock sector during prolonged droughts.

\## 4

The Strategic Value of Early Sowing

A recurring "Rule of Thumb" among Moroccan agronomists is the importance of
early sowing

(semis précoce).

\## 9

By planting in November, wheat and barley can establish their root systems

before the coldest months and complete their flowering stage before the onset
of the

devastating Chergui winds in May.

\## 9

This practice is increasingly facilitated by the "Semis

Direct" technique, which allows for planting into dry soil or immediately after
the first rains

without time-consuming tillage.

Agronomic Logic in Legume Management

A critical "Rule of Thumb" for legumes is the management of sowing depth. Large
seeds like

fava beans require a depth of $6 - 10 cm$ to ensure adequate moisture contact,
whereas

smaller seeds like lentils are sown at $4 - 6 cm$.

\## 31

Furthermore, the application of a small

"starter dose" of nitrogen ($10 - 20 kg/ha$) at sowing is recommended to
support early

growth until the symbiotic nodules become functional.

To effectively transform this data into a functional RAG-based recommendation

\## Strategic Chunking Logic

A standard character-count split often fragments critical agronomic
relationships (e.g.,

separating a crop's soil requirement from its water stress period). The agent
should employ

Semantic Chunking based on the following parameters:

\- Chunk Size (~1000–1200 tokens): This size is sufficient to encapsulate the
complete

technical profile of one crop or a detailed description of a soil type and its
associated

management practices.

\## 31

\- Overlap (~200 tokens): This ensures that the crop name and regional context
are

preserved across adjacent chunks, allowing the agent to maintain focus during
complex

queries about "disease management in specific soils".

\## 16

\- Entity-Centric Splitting: Chunks should ideally break at the beginning of a
new crop

section or a major technical heading (e.g., "Exigences Pédoclimatiques") to
maintain the

coherence of the advice.

Synthesis and Recommendations for Future Iterations

The creation of a RAG-based recommendation agent for Morocco is not merely a
technical

challenge but an exercise in agronomic synthesis. The insights derived from the
INRA bulletins

and FAO datasets indicate that the "one-size-fits-all" approach to agriculture
is defunct. The

agent must be capable of navigating the subtle differences between a Tirs soil
in the Gharb

and a Hamri soil in the Saïs to provide accurate irrigation and varietal advice.

\## 12

Furthermore, the agent must be a "living" system. As the "Génération Green"
strategy

progresses, new varieties like Irchad (Blé tendre) or Raeda (Fève) will replace
older

standards, and the agent's vector database must be updated to reflect these
genetic gains in

resilience.

\## 26

The integration of real-time meteorological data (NDVI, VHI, Precipitation) as

metadata will allow the agent to move from static advice to dynamic, predictive

warnings—alerting a citrus farmer in Taroudant of an impending Chergui and
recommending a

specific irrigation schedule to minimize fruit drop.

\## 5

In conclusion, by strictly adhering to semantic chunking, localized metadata
filtering, and the

integration of both high-level research data and vernacular expert logic, the
proposed RAG

agent can serve as a cornerstone of Morocco's digital agricultural sovereignty,
ensuring that

every farmer has an agronomist's expertise in their pocket.
